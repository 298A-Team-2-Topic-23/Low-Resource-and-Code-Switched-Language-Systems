# HinGE reference count: 4,799 measured against 4,803 published

Linear **298-77**. Investigated 9 November 2026 by Shevkar, Yash. Regenerate with:

```bash
python scripts/audit_hinge_count.py data/raw/HinGE.pkl \
    --expected-sha256 e78c8f48af3eea0b141e767082189b7b619e9cbe50ad8f0b2a4863b82ebc5960 \
    --csv data/raw/hinge.csv \
    --hinglisheval data/raw/hinglisheval/human_generated
```

Machine-readable result: `reports/hinge_reference_count.json`, with counts and row indices only and
no corpus text.

## Verdict

**4,799 is the correct count for the released file, and the pipeline reads every reference in it.**
The four-reference gap is not a parsing loss, so **no downstream number needs re-running**. The
attrition table, the splits, section 3.6 and the frozen manifest all stand. The published 4,803
cannot be reproduced from either release the authors made. The paper is also internally
inconsistent on the pair count, which is the most likely origin of the gap. We report both figures
and use 4,799, since it is the only one anyone can check.

This is a documented dead end. The cause sits on the authors' side, in a version of the data that
was never released, and cannot be confirmed without them.

## What was checked

The release used is `HinGE.pkl`, SHA-256 `e78c8f48…5960`. It was downloaded again from the authors'
Drive folder on 9 November and matches the hash pinned in `docs/datasheet.md`. It was opened only
through the restricted unpickler in `scripts/convert_hinge_pkl.py`.

The candidates from the 298-77 brief, cheapest first:

| # | Candidate cause | Result | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Empty or whitespace-only references that ingest drops | **Ruled out.** None exist | 0 of 4,799 references are blank |
| 2 | Duplicate references inside one row: the paper counts positions, we count distinct strings | **Ruled out, and in the wrong direction.** We count positions too. One row (index 310) repeats a reference, so counting distinct strings would give 4,798, further from 4,803 | `distinct_references_per_row_summed` = 4,798 |
| 3 | Malformed cells where the parser silently returns fewer items | **Ruled out.** All 1,976 cells are Python lists of strings, and the row index runs 0 to 1,975 with no gaps | `cells_not_a_list` = 0; `non_string_references` = 0 |
| 4 | Our ingest counting differently from the file | **Ruled out.** Stage 1 reads 4,799 records from the converted CSV, exactly the 4,799 list positions in the pickle | `pipeline_ingest.records` = 4,799 |
| 5 | Pairs the authors counted but left out of `HinGE.pkl` | **Ruled out for both releases.** The authors' second release, HinglishEval (same Drive folder), covers the same 1,973 unique English sources. Its one apparently extra source is a corrupted copy of a HinGE source (`front s4kin` for `front skin`, Jaccard 0.95) | `hinglisheval_crosscheck` |
| 6 | The published figure counts something the release does not contain | **The only candidate left, and the paper supports it.** See below | Paper, Table 1 and section 2 |

## Why the paper is the likely source

The released file matches the paper on every other figure in Table 1, which is what makes the human
count stand out:

| Table 1 figure | Paper | Release | Match |
|---|---|---|---|
| Parallel sentences | 1,976 | 1,976 rows | Yes |
| Machine-generated Hinglish sentences | 3,952 | 3,952 non-empty WAC + PAC cells (2 × 1,976) | Yes |
| Human-generated Hinglish sentences | 4,803 | 4,799 | **No, 4 short** |

The paper also contradicts itself on the pair count. Section 2 says the authors "obtain **1,978**
sentence pairs with two or more unique Hinglish sentences", while Table 1 gives **1,976**. That
sentence makes two further claims the release does not satisfy:

- **"Two or more" references per pair.** In the release, 23 of 1,976 rows have exactly one
  reference.
- **"Unique" references.** One row repeats a reference.

Two pairs at two references each, the minimum the paper describes, is exactly four references. A
count taken over 1,978 pairs, before two pairs were removed for the release, would give
4,799 + 4 = 4,803. **This fits every observation, but it is an inference, not a confirmation.**
The two removed pairs are not in either release, so their reference count cannot be checked. Asking
the authors is the only way to confirm it, and it is not worth doing for a 0.08% difference that
changes no conclusion.

## Consequences

- **No re-run.** Every downstream figure was computed from the 4,799 references actually in the
  file. Section 3.6, the attrition table and the split manifest are unaffected. On 9 November the
  splits regenerated byte-identical from a fresh download (section 3.5.6).
- **How to quote it.** "4,799 human references, measured off the released file; the paper reports
  4,803, a figure not reproducible from either released file (`reports/hinge_reference_count.md`)."
- **Two corrections to how the corpus is described.** "Every pair has at least two references" is
  not true of the release, since 23 rows have one. "The released file has 1,973 unique English
  sources", not 1,976, because three sentences appear twice with separate annotations. Both were
  already measured in `reports/data_statistics.json` (`references_per_source`) and
  `docs/datasheet.md`. They are listed here because a reader comparing against the paper would
  otherwise find them.
