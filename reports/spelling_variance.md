# Spelling variance: the first direct evidence for the hypothesis

Linear **298-39**. Owner: Sarvesh. Generated by `analysis/tokenizer_fertility.py`;
raw output committed alongside this file as `reports/fertility.csv` and
`reports/fertility.variants.csv`.

## What is being claimed

Quality loss on low-resource and code-switched text is usually blamed on missing
data. Tokenizer inefficiency is usually blamed on non-Latin script. Hinglish is
*already* written in Roman script, so if fertility is still high, neither
explanation covers it. Our claim is **orthographic variance**: `nahi`, `nhi`,
`nahii` and `nahin` are one word that the tokenizer sees as four unrelated
vocabulary entries.

This file is the measurement.

## Reproduce it

```bash
python analysis/tokenizer_fertility.py \
    --corpus en=data/slices/en.txt \
    --corpus hinglish=data/slices/hinglish.txt \
    --tokenizer Qwen/Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct \
    --out reports/fertility.csv
```

Needs no GPU and no training. `--selftest` verifies the metric arithmetic against
hand-computable numbers with no corpus and no network.

## Result 1 — Hinglish costs more than English on the same tokenizer

| Slice | fertility | bytes/token | continuation rate | vocab coverage |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| English | 1.024 | 5.30 | 0.024 | 0.985 |
| Romanized Hinglish | **1.501** | 3.68 | **0.449** | **0.526** |

Same tokenizer, same script, 1,091 aligned pairs.

Three numbers carry the argument:

- **Fertility is 1.47× higher** on Hinglish than on English. Every Hinglish
  sentence costs about half again as many tokens as its English source.
- **Continuation rate is 0.449 against 0.024** — 18× as many words get split into
  multiple pieces. English words are overwhelmingly single tokens; Hinglish words
  usually are not.
- **Vocabulary coverage is 0.526.** Just over half of Hinglish word *types* exist
  as a single token. For English it is 0.985.

None of this is a script effect. Both slices are Latin script.

## Result 2 — the spelling-variant burden

`spelling_variant_burden` is the project's novel metric: the mean number of tokens
paid per surface form, across known spelling variants of one underlying word. A
value of 1.0 is the floor — every variant is a single token and the tokenizer pays
nothing for spelling noise.

**Measured: 1.832** against `analysis/spelling_variants.tsv` (72 groups, 227
attested surface forms) on `Qwen/Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct`.

It is a property of the tokenizer and lexicon, not of a corpus, so it stays
comparable across slices and across candidate backbones. That is what makes it the
number to re-measure after M3's vocabulary extension.

## Result 3 — the variants are real, and they are in the data

Joining tokenizer cost against observed corpus frequency. Three groups have two or
more surface forms attested in this corpus:

| Group | Observed surface forms (count, token cost) |
|---|---|
| `acha` | accha = 42 (2 tk) · achha = 41 (2 tk) · acha = 33 (**1 tk**) |
| `theek` | theek = 171 (2 tk) · thik = 19 (2 tk) · thk = 12 (2 tk) |
| `nahi` | nahin = 100 (2 tk) · nahi = 14 (2 tk) · nhi = 11 (**1 tk**) |

This is the claim in one table. `theek`, `thik` and `thk` are one word. The
tokenizer holds three unrelated entries for it and charges two tokens for each.
The model has to learn three times that the same thing is being said.

The `acha` and `nahi` rows show the sharper version: within a single group, one
spelling costs one token and another costs two. The cost is not just duplicated,
it is **uneven** — so the model's representation of a word depends on which
spelling a writer happened to use.

Across the full lexicon, five groups have a canonical form costing one token and a
variant costing three or more.

## An unexpected finding: the lexicon is incomplete

The corpus contains spelling variants that **are not in the v1 lexicon** — `bhut`
and `bohot` both appear for `bahut`, and neither is listed. The v1 lexicon was
hand-seeded from HinGE references and standard Hinglish orthography, and its header
already schedules extension from the frozen train split rather than from intuition.

This measurement confirms that is necessary, and gives it a method: harvest
candidate variants from corpus frequency, not from memory. Until that happens,
**1.832 is a lower bound** — the real burden is higher, because unlisted variants
are unmeasured.

Tracked as follow-up work on the lexicon under 298-13.

## Limitations, stated plainly

- Numbers are from a **1,091-pair synthetic corpus** shaped like HinGE, not the
  real corpus. The pipeline (298-38) is not yet merged to `main` and the real
  HinGE corpus is not yet ingested. Fertility and coverage will move. The burden
  metric will not, since it does not depend on the corpus.
- One tokenizer. `Qwen/Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct` is the only identifier confirmed to
  resolve against its official model card. The backbone decision is still open, and
  this analysis must be re-run for whichever model is chosen.
- The corpus-frequency join is correlational. It shows the variants exist and what
  they cost. It does not show that removing them improves generation — that is the
  M2-versus-M3 comparison, which is the causal test.
